Privacy Charter

Privacy Boundaries & Anonymity Disclosures

A transparent explanation of what information is collected, how anonymity is protected, and the legal limits of confidentiality.

1. The Principle of Proportional Collection

redFlag collects only the minimum information necessary to triage a civic grievance or incident allegation. You are never required to link your legal name, social identity, or permanent residency to file a report.

2. How Anonymous Reporting Works

When you check the "Remain Anonymous" box on the reporting form:

  • All telephone numbers and email addresses are wiped from the form payload prior to persistence in our database.
  • The report is decoupled from any authenticated user account.
  • Your generated reference code (e.g., RF-2026-XXXXXX) serves as the sole token to check case updates. We have no mechanism to contact you once you leave the platform unless you provide voluntary contact details.

3. Limits of Anonymity

We do not make misleading claims of mathematical or absolute invisibility. If you include self-identifying statements inside your incident description, or if your uploaded photographs contain visible metadata or personal identifiers, investigators may be able to deduce context.

Furthermore, redFlag complies with valid judicial subpoenas and court orders requiring the production of server logs in cases involving extreme crimes (such as child sexual exploitation or imminent acts of terrorism).

4. Protection of Uploaded Evidence

Uploaded photographs, audio recordings, or documents are stored in private filesystem directories outside the web server's public document root. Direct URL indexing and public downloads are strictly forbidden. Access is gated by server-side permission checks requiring authenticated staff credentials.

5. Data Retention & Deletion

Resolved and closed cases are retained for statutory audit compliance for a standard period of 36 months, after which associated media attachments are scrubbed from disk storage.
